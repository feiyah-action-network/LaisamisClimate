## 2. Application answers

> TPP only shows the full question list after you pass the quiz. These answers cover the standard fields. Copy them into the form and trim to its word limits.

**Project name**
Laisamis Community Land Paralegals: Ward Training Day

**One-sentence summary**
Training 10 local young people, at least half of them women, as community land paralegals so their pastoralist communities in Laisamis, northern Kenya, can register and protect their own land under Kenya's Community Land Act.

**Where**
[WARD] Ward, Laisamis Constituency, Marsabit County, Kenya

**What problem are you addressing?**
Ten years after Kenya's Community Land Act (2016), only about 64 of an estimated 1,000 community land units nationally have registered title. In Laisamis Constituency, about 1.85 million hectares used by Rendille, Samburu, Gabbra, El Molo and Turkana pastoralists is legally unregistered. Without registration, families face land disputes, exclusion from decisions about their own land, and deals made without their consent, while drought and erratic rain make secure land more important than ever. Most people here have never been told what the Act says or how registration works, and women, who rarely speak at land meetings, are told least of all.

**What will you do?**
Over one day, I will train 10 young volunteers from [WARD] Ward, at least five of them women, as community land paralegals. They'll learn the registration steps under the Community Land Act, how free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) works, and how to record a community decision properly. A few days later, the paralegals will help run a ward assembly where the community hears, in its own language, what registration means and records its questions and decisions in a signed consent register. Each paralegal gets a simple Kiswahili guide and a record book so they can keep answering their neighbours' questions after the grant is spent.

**Why you? (your story)**
I was born and raised in Laisamis. My late father, Ntepesi Nalle, was a community leader, and I grew up watching people come to him with land disputes that a clear registration process would have prevented. For more than ten years I have organised youth and women in Marsabit through the Feiyah Action Network, a volunteer-run women's rights community group. In 2026 I started the Laisamis Climate and Land Initiative (LCLI) to focus on land and climate. We have already held nine National Land Commission sensitisation forums across the constituency. What's missing is local people who can carry this knowledge between meetings. That's what this grant builds.

**Who benefits, and how many?**
- 10 trained paralegals (at least 5 women, aged 18–35) directly
- About [60] residents of [WARD] Ward who attend the assembly
- Indirectly, every household in the ward that relies on this land for grazing and water

**How will you measure success?**
- 10 paralegals trained (attendance sheet, at least 50% women)
- 1 ward assembly held, with a signed consent register and minutes
- Number of residents reached (sign-in sheet, split by gender)
- Within 60 days: number of questions or cases paralegals handled (from their record books)
- Photos from the training and the assembly for the report to TPP

**What have you already done?**
Nine National Land Commission sensitisation forums across Laisamis Constituency, working relationships with the NLC and Marsabit County Government, a concept note, and a public website: laisamisclimate.co.ke

**Timeline**
- Weeks 1–2 after funds arrive: select paralegals with ward elders and women's groups; print materials
- Week 3: training day
- Week 4: ward assembly
- Week 6: report and photos sent to TPP

**Links**
